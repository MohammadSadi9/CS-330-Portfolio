# CS-330 Portfolio

## Computer Graphics and Visualization

This repository contains my CS-330 coursework, including my 3D scene project created using C++, OpenGL, and the provided graphics framework. The project represents a desk setup with a monitor and decorative objects, demonstrating 3D transformations, primitive shapes, textures, colors, lighting, and camera controls.

## 3D Scene Project

The scene includes a wooden desk, a monitor with a frame, screen, stand, and base, along with several decorative objects. I used a rustic wood texture for the desk and different colors to distinguish the monitor components and accessories. The scene combines basic geometric primitives to create recognizable objects in a three-dimensional environment.

The project helped me understand how individual meshes can be scaled, positioned, and combined to form a complete scene. It also gave me practical experience with rendering, materials, lighting, and camera navigation.

## Design Decisions

My main design goal was to create a recognizable desk setup using simple geometric shapes. I chose a desk and monitor because they allowed me to demonstrate how multiple primitives can work together to create a real-world object. I used contrasting colors and a wood texture to make the scene easier to understand visually.

I organized the scene by setting transformations for each object and adjusting its position along the X, Y, and Z axes. I also worked with the project's camera and lighting features to support viewing the scene from different angles. The accompanying Design Decisions document explains these choices in more detail.

## Reflection

### 1. How do you approach designing software, and how has your approach evolved throughout the milestones?

My approach to software design begins with understanding the requirements and breaking a larger problem into smaller, manageable parts. In the 3D scene project, I first considered the overall arrangement of the desk and monitor, then identified the shapes needed to represent each component. I used transformations to control the scale and position of the individual objects.

As I progressed through the milestones, I developed a better understanding of how planning and organization affect the final result. Instead of treating the scene as one large object, I learned to think about it as a collection of components that can be developed and adjusted independently. This approach made it easier to refine the scene, troubleshoot issues, and make changes without redesigning everything.

### 2. How do you approach developing programs, and what role did iteration play in your development?

I approach program development by implementing a feature, testing it, reviewing the result, and making adjustments where necessary. In the 3D scene project, I used this process to adjust object dimensions, positions, colors, and textures until the components formed a coherent desk setup. Working with the graphics framework also helped me understand the importance of checking how code changes affect the rendered output.

Iteration was important because the first version of a scene does not always look or behave as expected. Testing and refining the program helped me identify problems with object placement and improve the overall composition. I also learned that successful development involves more than writing code: project organization, dependencies, testing, and documentation are important parts of delivering a complete program.
